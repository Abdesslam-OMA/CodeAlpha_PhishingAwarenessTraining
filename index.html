01. Understanding Phishing

Know the threat before you can defend against it.

What is phishing?

Phishing is a cyberattack in which attackers impersonate trusted individuals or organizations to trick people into revealing sensitive information, clicking malicious links, or downloading harmful files.


📧

Email Phishing

Fraudulent emails pretending to come from legitimate companies or people.

📱

Smishing

Phishing attacks delivered through SMS or text messages.

🎯

Spear Phishing

Targeted attacks designed for a specific person or organization.

🌐

Website Phishing

Fake websites designed to imitate legitimate login pages.

02. Recognizing Phishing Emails

Learn to identify common warning signs.

⚠️ Suspicious sender

The sender address may contain misspellings or an unfamiliar domain.

⚠️ Urgent language

Messages pressure you to act immediately or threaten account suspension.

⚠️ Unexpected links

Links may lead to fraudulent websites that steal login credentials.

⚠️ Unexpected attachments

Unknown attachments may contain malware or harmful files.

Example: A suspicious email

From: Security Alert <security@example.invalid>

Subject: URGENT! Your account will be closed!

Dear Customer,

Your account has been temporarily restricted. Verify your identity immediately or your account will be deleted.

[Suspicious verification link — demonstration only]

Thank you,
Account Security Team

What makes this suspicious?

  • Generic greeting instead of your name.
  • Threatening and urgent language.
  • Unfamiliar sender address.
  • Request to verify sensitive information.

Remember: Never use suspicious links to access your accounts.

03. Identifying Fake Websites

Look-alike domains

Attackers may use misspelled or misleading domain names.

Fake login pages

Fraudulent pages copy the appearance of legitimate services to steal credentials.

Check the URL

Inspect the complete domain carefully before entering any information.

HTTPS is not enough

A secure connection does not prove that a website is legitimate.

04. Social Engineering

Attackers often manipulate human behavior rather than technology.

Fear

Threatening account closure or financial loss to force a quick response.

Urgency

Creating artificial deadlines to prevent careful thinking.

Authority

Pretending to be a manager, bank employee, or IT administrator.

Curiosity

Using attractive offers or surprising messages to encourage clicking.

05. How to Stay Protected

Simple habits can significantly reduce phishing risks.

  • Verify the sender before trusting an email.
  • Inspect links before opening them.
  • Never share passwords or verification codes.
  • Enable multi-factor authentication (MFA).
  • Use strong, unique passwords.
  • Keep your operating system and applications updated.
  • Use official websites and trusted applications.
  • Report suspicious messages to the appropriate security team.

What if you clicked a phishing link?

  1. Stop interacting with the suspicious website.
  2. Do not enter any more personal information.
  3. If you shared a password, change it immediately using the official website.
  4. Enable MFA and contact the relevant service provider or IT team.
  5. Report the incident and follow your organization's security procedures.
